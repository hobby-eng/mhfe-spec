# AUD-012 — Lean proofs: validity, exact correspondence and publication readiness

## Assessment

All 39 current mathematical chapters and the package entry point passed fresh direct-source
checking. The resulting 40 module artifacts passed separate kernel replay. The checkpoint found
6,036 local mathematical declarations, including 3,888 theorems, whose transitive axioms are only
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. No false final numerical inequality was identified
under the statements' actual hypotheses. The new K5 loss and W5 generation-budget exception agree

**Literal full correspondence to the unrestricted specification is not established.** In particular,
the final Theorem 2 game restricts genuine-wallet records more than the specification and the

W5/W6/W7 endpoints still require finite passphrase alphabets. Several exact ancillary statements
also remain unassembled or informally interpreted. These are distinguished from false checked
theorems throughout this report.

**Publication recommendation:** correct the kernel-check loop and the scope/trust descriptions
before publication. Extend the deniability game if the claim is the full experiment of the text;
otherwise identify the narrower formal experiment explicitly. The current evidence supports useful
checked formal results with stated boundaries. No proof, specification, dependency or workflow was
changed, and no commit, push or release was made.

#### Semantic foundations

##### Typed functions, state widths and independent tables

`OracleModel.lean:108`–`OracleModel.lean:158` defines the suite-3 state as two `BitVec 128`
halves, 128-bit salts, 256-bit keys and hash outputs. Its tagged `Point` type distinguishes A, B,
H and G, and `spec` assigns the right output type to each query. `Tables` has four separate full
functions. `sampleTables` at line 316 samples the finite function tables independently, and
`TableFactors.sample_uniform`/`factor_a`/`factor_b`/`factor_h` prove the factorings later used
for fresh-point reasoning. This is a random-function model, not an implementation of Argon2id,
BLAKE2b or HMAC.

`execute` at `OracleModel.lean:153` evaluates an adaptive VCVio oracle computation against
consistent fixed tables and logs every query, including repetitions. The adversary receives the
container rather than the tables or real password. Its continuation depends on query answers;
the full tables occur in the experiment, not in its parameter list. Hard budgets quantify over
every container and every table, not merely their average under the sampled experiment.

`DeniabilityModel.lean:15`–`DeniabilityModel.lean:55` gives the width-parametric version: the
half width changes between suites while salts remain 128 bits and keys remain 256 bits. The
round index is `Fin 12` in both models. `forward` and `finalize` use the actual Feistel recurrence;
`forward_cipher` and `inverse_cipher` identify their executions with the proved permutations.

##### Deterministic programs, private coins and termination

The base `OracleComp` evaluator is deterministic given its full tables and program. It is a total
well-founded oracle program; it is not a program-logic model with a possibly diverging while
loop. Crypto answers range over finite types, so a fixed such strategy has finite query branching.
The deniability record type is abstract rather than a fixed finite bit width; its total program
and PMF-record model still do not expose a divergence event.

Randomness is not silently omitted. Explicit PMF mixtures of independently sampled strategies
occur in `G3.lemma_g3` (`ChainEarly.lean:822`), `G4.randomized`
(`ChainCompletion.lean:452`), `Theorem3Randomized.bound` (`ShortSources.lean:983`),
`G6.randomized` (line 1260), `G7Randomized.bound` (`BatchQueries.lean:566`),
`G10Coins.randomized` (`WalletData.lean:872`), the G10 refusal variants, and
`Theorem2.averaged_bound` (`DeniabilityGames.lean:1446`). Each strategy must satisfy the hard
budgets. Query choices may still depend on all previous oracle answers.

An arbitrary distribution on strategies may mix arbitrarily large finite strategies; that is
different from assuming one global finite internal-computation bound. Nevertheless, the source
does not export a semantics-preserving translation of every arbitrary possibly nonterminating
or almost-surely terminating probabilistic algorithm into this strategy-mixture model. Therefore
the safe checked claim is about these total oracle computations and their explicit independent
PMF mixtures. The text's unrestricted “when it halts” wording needs a stated convention or an
additional lifting theorem if literal operational completeness is claimed.

##### Ranked password domains

`RankedMass.top` (`ShortSources.lean:134`) is the mass of the first `m` labels in `Fin N`,
saturating at N. In the upper bounds `sorted : Antitone passwords` makes these the most likely
labels. `p1` is an explicit upper bound on every mass and can be chosen as the actual largest
mass. The lower matching bounds need no sorted hypothesis because they hold for any chosen
injective candidate order; interpreting their prefix as the most likely candidates additionally
uses the ranked labeling.

`PasswordDomains.lean` provides exact recoding of finite password labels, tables, executions and
budgets, and a Theorem-3 transport at line 222. Its `admitted_finite` at line 324 proves finiteness
of any admitted subset of bounded byte encodings. This addresses the distinction between the full

#### Important details behind the table

##### G1, G2 and the finalization bridge

The G1 equality is an explicit finite bijection exchanging the free hash-verifier bits with an
extra uniform verifier value. Its observation retains the whole G table and arbitrary environment,
so it is not merely a uniformity observation about Y. The original theorem game maps the same
coordinate resampling into the actual Feistel cipher and actual attacker execution.

G2's `Run` records all queries, the attack halt index, the real chain and the assertion that
finalization eventually queries every chain point. `fromTrace` builds it from attack queries
followed by the real inverse chain. `Early` is defined by the first query's time being earlier
than the time the chain point becomes determined; recursive max handles a predecessor that was
itself queried early. The order and missing-later lemmas use actual first occurrences, not an
assumption that the attack already queried the chain sequentially.

##### G3/G4/G5 and the principal term

The first-Early decomposition uses fresh sampled answers at stopping points, rather than
conditioning without justification on future attacker choices. Partitioning by round retains
the single qB and qH budgets; an A query has no round tag, so it is exposed to all twelve salts.
The self-chain contribution is the exact sum of preceding A counts, 66.

G4 first proves a half-match statement and then the actual full-entropy G-query event using the
projection premise that E contains L0. `Complete` asks only that the twelve A points were queried;
G2 gives the missing final H point when this is false. G5 injects twelve distinct A-chain points
into actual attack queries. Candidate counting bounds the number of passwords with twelve A calls,
and the selected set is independent of P in the plain game. Those dependencies are proved facts,
not security premises supplied to the final inequality.

##### Batch model

`BatchProgram.Program` at `BatchQueries.lean:62` commits a list of points and receives a reply
cache only after the entire list was submitted. The initial cache is fixed; its entries at
unqueried points remain fixed defaults, not secret table values. `BatchFetch.execution` gives
the exact cache and original multiplicities. `BatchProgram.execution` compiles each committed
list with A points first and proves output equality and the reordered transcript. This is a
genuine mathematical scheduling reduction for mixed A/B/H/G batches. It does not assume the
attacker is naturally A-first or that cheap queries are sequential by fiat.

The formal final bound therefore corresponds to the second route now explicitly stated in the
text. The first route's stronger statement using a batch-extended Early event is not separately
proved, and the port README already says so.

##### Matching attacks and ENNReal subtraction

The matching sources prove actual program outcomes and budgets, not just existence of a candidate
test. All expensive work occurs through the same logged oracle interface as the upper bounds.
The false-pass estimate separates entropy collisions from a fresh verifier comparison, giving
the expected delta. With wallet data, the verifier and wallet comparisons are jointly uniform,
giving 2^-(r+lambda), not a sum of their false-pass probabilities.

The lower bounds use ENNReal subtraction, which truncates below zero. Where a text expression
`1-j*delta` would be negative, the formal expression is zero. This is a stronger nonnegative
lower bound, not a literal equality with the real-valued expression on those parameters. It
implies the text's inequality. Where the bound is useful and every loss is at most one, the
expressions agree exactly. This convention is disclosed in Chapters/README and must be preserved
in the final audit's precision claims.

At m=0 the named attacks can still query the common first salt, within the bound qB≤1, but do no
Argon2 work. For requested m beyond the finite dictionary, `PasswordPrefix` tests only the
available prefix and saturates `top`, so unlike an impossible candidate list the statement stays
meaningful for every m. G10's named attack still queries G even with an empty verifier; removing
that unused call would give a smaller cheap-query budget and is not required for the claimed
current caps.

##### Wallet-data interpretation

`G10AsDirect.original` proves an exact whole-game identity from independent entropy-based Omega
to the direct phrase-domain model with identity encoding. This does not incorrectly identify
a G-dependent composition with an independent Omega table. `G10Direct` proves the checksum-aware
upper bound first; `DerivedWalletMatching` separately proves the composition-law identity and

bits. `execution` and `counts` retain the actual G call used to derive that checksum. The result

finite support.

`Category window` retains each window label and maps all outside prior mass to a single Other
category. `some_mass` preserves every individual inside probability, and `none_mass` preserves the
entire outside tail. The source-specific embedding distinguishes the actual Q row even when Q is
outside the queried window. `native` samples the existing `WalletAt.actual` semantics on
`insert real window`, so the generation reads the true source row rather than a guessed row.

`native_eq` proves the native experiment equals the category experiment for the covered program.
`conditional_between` and `original_between` prove that enlarging the hidden window changes no
selected-output law. `finite_window` uses the vanishing PMF tail, and the final bounds pass to the
limit with `ENNReal.le_of_forall_pos_le_add`. Pair success additionally includes every emitted
passphrase in the output window so that Other cannot impersonate a guessed native value.

This justifies the arbitrary-Q main T5 endpoint. It does not by itself give a checked arbitrary-Q
W5 or W6 output endpoint, a countable W7 matching-attack source endpoint, or the known-Q/full-cost
family under arbitrary priors. Those still use their declared finite alphabets.

##### W5's delta_gen exception survives the complete proof path

`W5Generation.actual` observes A's output from the actual unbounded source generation.
`W5Generation.bound` applies `FullGenerationCoupling.bound` to A itself with its original kS and
qT. Its score compares the output to the selected entropy/passphrase; no S query is added at that
generation step.

`WalletOutputQuery.augment` later appends an S query at the candidate output. `output_member`
proves the output appears in the augmented S-query transcript. The programming-removal transfer
then bounds a correct entropy/passphrase output by the Bad-only event of the augmented program,
rather than by a seed-winning event. `W5Source.bound` invokes the raw bound at kS+1 but adds
`W2.delta kS qT`. `W5.error` adds only the first-refusal rate. This proves exactly the text's
current rule and does not accept a different entropy yielding the same seed.

##### Selection rules and the empty passing set

`Policy : List Bool -> PMF (Option Nat)` describes outcome-dependent continuation or choosing an
earlier trial. `weight` is the operational product of uniform trial probabilities, all prior
continue decisions and the final chosen index. `HasSum weight 1` excludes invalid selections and
divergence. Invariance under permutations of the passing entropies establishes uniform selection;
the proof does not posit the desired chosen-entropy law.

For an empty passing set, `chosenLaw` uses the same arbitrary uniform selected-triple fallback as
`WalletCompletion.real`. This is the text's allowed arbitrary outcome on the exceptional branch,
not an assumption that a passing value always exists. The whole source/view generation difference
is bounded by W2 with its actual tail/collision accounting.

The final hidden-policy theorem is an equality of selected triple and adversarial transcript laws.
Its operational policy has no access to the actual trial values. A table-indexed family of
policies is even allowed; at fixed tables it still sees only trial outcomes. No owner trial history
or duration is passed into the actual attack.

##### W4 and the assignment

`W4Model.Collision` quantifies over an actual different password whose fully queried chain completes
at or before the real chain and whose inverse state agrees. Its proof divides strictly earlier
completion from simultaneous completion. The two bounds charge a fresh final mask hit among at
most kA earlier completed candidates and a final-key equality among at most kA A answers. Other
password chains need no ordered-query or fresh-salt assumptions. `W4Wallet` preserves additional
S/T/Omega query execution and averages any independent wallet context.

PairAssignment maps every collected S query to the earliest completed eligible password row,
breaking ties by rank. The query set has at most kS elements, and completed rows consume twelve
real A queries. The resulting set is a function of the run and can be bounded by W1 after sampling
P,Q independently; the proof does not choose the pair set after observing the secrets.

##### W7 modes, collisions and nonnegative subtraction

The operational attack evaluates the shared public last-round B input once, inverts each row with
the same salt, caches its entropy, then queries S and its selected verifier. Both mode execution
and the exact transcript are proved; the probability argument's effective verifier abstraction
does not hide an Omega query on a failed check. `both_omega_count` counts passing T answers even
when the subsequent Omega test fails.

The seed game wins when an earlier wrong pair produces the real seed, so its lower bound has only
the effective-verifier false-acceptance loss. The pair game loses in that case and pays both the
2^-512 accidental seed loss and the entropy-alias term for another password row. Entropy collisions
are charged once per other password, not once per passphrase.

The text uses real subtraction in its lower bounds. Lean uses ENNReal truncated subtraction,
including clamping a factor at zero when its loss exceeds one. That yields a stronger nonnegative
lower statement in vacuous regimes and agrees with the text when losses are below one. This is a
valid probability convention, not an omitted negative probability or a changed error constant.

4. **W7 assembled bracket and at-most-n form.** Final source lower bounds use an injective

5. **Capped generation reporting failure.** Existing finite search returns an Option and its

are false. They should not be presented as cryptographic attacks or as defects in the Rust tool.

#### Fractional-top characterization: partly proved, no optimizer endpoint found

SPEC 4384-4387 makes three precise claims in addition to defining the formula:
for every real x >= 0, T(x) is the maximum weighted mass under fractional capacity x;
the maximum is attained by greedily filling the highest probabilities; and
top(floor x) <= T(x) <= top(ceil x).

The current Lean development proves the upper-bound ingredient used by Theorem 4:

- `L/KnownPairBound.lean:23-24` defines `FractionalMass.leadingMass` as the finite
  prefix sum. At 27-50, `FractionalMass.greedy` proves a universal inequality for
  *any supplied* weight vector: weights in [0,1] with total at most `capacity`
  have weighted mass at most `leadingMass p whole + (capacity-whole)*p whole`.
  Its proof is a pointwise comparison using antitonicity, followed by summation
  and the supplied budget. There is no constructed optimizing vector in this proof.
- `L/KnownPairBound.lean:58-61` defines `FractionalTop.value p whole part` by the
  desired prefix-plus-fraction formula and saturates at the full dictionary mass.
  At 64-75, `FractionalTop.bound` transfers the preceding universal upper bound
  to this formula, with `whole : Nat` and `part : Real` as separate parameters.
  At 78-87, `known_pair` instantiates these parameters to `kA/11+1` and
  `(kA%11)/11`. The arithmetic identity required by the actual Theorem 4
  application is proved there.
- `L/KnownPairBound.lean:482-484` embeds this exact finite formula into ENNReal
  as `KnownPairMass.value`. Thus the Theorem 4 main upper endpoint uses the
  intended fractional expression, not a weaker integer substitute.
- `L/KnownPairBound.lean:904-916` proves the prefix-sum successor identity.
  At 918-933, `FractionalTopBounds.upper` proves
  `value p whole part <= leadingMass p (whole+1)` when `part <= 1` and the
  probabilities are nonnegative. At 935-942 it identifies the real prefix sum
  with `RankedMass.top`, and at 945-952 derives the stated coarser Theorem 4
  bound `KnownPairMass.value <= top(kA/11+2)`.

After searching all current chapter sources for these definitions and their uses,
and inspecting `Probability.lean` and `ShortSources.lean`, I found no theorem
constructing weights with the budget and exact mass `FractionalTop.value`, no
`IsGreatest`/maximum/supremum characterization of the fractional feasible set,
and no general real-x floor/ceiling interface. I also found no explicit lower
prefix bound `leadingMass p whole <= FractionalTop.value p whole part` under
`0 <= part`, nor the combined floor/ceil sandwich.

`L/ShortSources.lean:103-124` does prove `top2_attained`, but its witness is a
finite set of password/passphrase pairs maximizing their *unfractional* product
mass. Its proof enumerates a finite family of pair sets and applies
`Finset.exists_max_image`; it neither constructs fractional password weights
nor mentions `FractionalTop.value`. `RankedMass.set_bound` at 138-164 is likewise
an ordinary finite-set bound. `Probability.lean` supplies moment, independence,
transcript-erasure and freshness machinery; it does not close this optimization
characterization.

**Verdict.** Confirm the absence of an explicitly checked fractional optimizer
and full lower-floor sandwich endpoint in the current proof corpus. Do not say
that fractional top or Theorem 4 is absent: the exact formula, the universal
upper inequality needed for it, and the advertised coarser upper consequence
are proved. The omitted optimizer and lower-floor statements are elementary
consequences of the finite formula, but identifying that mathematical derivation
is different from finding a kernel-checked theorem in the current port. This is
a completeness/documentation qualification, not a counterexample to a checked
Theorem 4 inequality.

A direct completion would define greedy weights (1 below `whole`, `part` at
`whole`, 0 above it, with saturation), prove feasibility and exact mass for
`part` in [0,1], expose the real-x decomposition, and package the sandwich.

#### Genuine-wallet record kernel: narrower than the full `dReal` reading

SPEC 552-559 restricts the disclosed-wallet scenario U to the phrase and
independent randomness. SPEC 621-625 then says the genuine wallet is used
"in any way". The EasyCrypt model makes its intended broader reading explicit:
`src/Deniability.ec:44-49` gives

```text
dReal : ent -> pw -> state -> state -> pw -> state -> record -> record distr
```

Its arguments include the decoy password, recovered decoy state and realized
decoy record. In the prepared experiment at 135-139, the decoy is recovered,
`rc` is sampled from `dU`, and only then `rr` is sampled from
`dReal e p x y q xd rc`. This permits the genuine record to depend on the
particular decoy and its independently generated U record.

Lean currently permits substantial genuine-record generality, but a smaller
one:

- `L/DeniabilityGames.lean:1090` and the final `Theorem2.bound` parameter at
  1427 have type
  `Password -> Entropy -> Tables Password Entropy -> PMF Record`.
  It sees the genuine password and entropy plus the complete original crypto
  tables. With fixed `pack` and public construction parameters it can therefore
  depend on X, Y, genuine round values and other functions of that context.
- `oldKernel` at 1092-1096 samples the genuine record first, then the disclosed
  password, then calls `opening`. `opening` at 516-520 recovers the disclosed
  state and samples its record from `usage` using that state. The real-record
  kernel cannot inspect either later realized sample.
- The actual `prepared` endpoint samples `disclosed` before `preparedOnce`
  (1271-1273), but `preparedOnce` at 1254-1259 still does not pass it to
  `realRecords`, and still samples the genuine record before U. The genuine
  record is conditionally independent of the accepted decoy password and U
  sample given the retained genuine context. Merely reordering the draws
  does not add the missing dependency.
- `Theorem2.averaged_bound` at 1446-1463 adds an outer independent `Setup` and
  permits `realRecords setup ...`. Its `prepared` call still performs fresh
  password and U sampling internally. This does not supply the later realized
  Q or U record to the genuine-record kernel. `bits` at 1468-1482 is a
  specialization of the same endpoint, so it preserves the restriction.
- `preparedLater` is arbitrary on first-refusal branches, but it is selected
  only when the refusal flag holds (1261-1273). It does not generalize the
  ordinary accepted branch.

The restriction is observable at the level of joint laws, rather than merely a
missing redundant argument. For example, fix a genuine context and distinct
genuine/disclosed phrases; let U return a fresh fair Boolean record independent
of the phrase. EasyCrypt allows `dReal(...,rc)` to return exactly `rc`, so the
two records agree with probability 1. Under Lean's current prepared kernel,
the genuine record is sampled independently before the fresh fair U Boolean,
so their equality has probability 1/2 regardless of its marginal. This is a
symbolic scope witness, not an attack or a claim that the advantage bound fails
under the more general model.

##### Existing generic proof ingredients do not yet provide a broader final game

I checked the relevant lower-level path instead of inferring absence solely
from `Theorem2.bound`'s type:

- `L/DeniabilityGames.lean:727-768` (`finish_agreement`) and 771-808
  (`same_if_clean`) hold for *arbitrary fixed values* `realRecord` and disclosed
  `record`, with no independence condition between these values. Their clean
  event is determined by the erased execution, which does not use the genuine
  record. This is exactly the useful ingredient for a future dependent-record
  extension.
- `opening_close` at 812-862 averages over U while keeping `realRecord` fixed.
  `conditional_close` at 1117-1134 next averages over the existing genuine-record
  kernel. It does not introduce a conditional kernel after the U sample.
- `L/Deniability.lean:48-58` constructs a programmable-record opening/simulator
  with a fixed base oracle supplied before password/U sampling; its redraw
  result at 145-150 uses that same opening. It is not an alternative full
  Theorem 2 endpoint with a genuine record generated from the realized decoy
  record.

The pointwise clean-run equality suggests the broader result should be obtainable
by a coupling over the U record followed by an arbitrary conditional genuine-record
kernel, because the latter remains unobserved until a charged real-phrase lookup.
That extension has not been assembled or kernel checked here, so it should not
be reported as an already established Lean theorem.

**Verdict.** Add a precise scope qualification to a claim that Lean proves the
full deniability game of the specification/EasyCrypt: the checked final Lean
game allows arbitrary genuine-record dependence on genuine P/E/oracles, but
not on the later realized decoy choice or U record. No false checked advantage
bound was identified. To remove the qualification, extend the accepted-branch
record kernel to the decoy Q/state/U record and lift the existing pointwise
agreement and refusal composition to that source-faithful game.
