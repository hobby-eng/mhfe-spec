# AUD-013 — EasyCrypt/Lean comparison and current EasyCrypt follow-up

## Assessment

The two developments support the same core numerical security bounds under their respective
formal hypotheses. Current K5 uses `2^-128 + 11·2^-256` in both; current W5 retains original `k_S`
only in `δ_gen` while charging the appended seed query elsewhere. No false final inequality was
identified. **They do not establish literally identical experiments or unrestricted coverage of
every sentence of the specification.** Their domains, adversary semantics, record kernels and
exported ancillary results differ.

The existing Claude comparison is useful and substantially careful. Its detailed sections already
disclose most important limitations, including the genuine-record restriction, finite passphrase
corollaries, missing fractional optimizer/expectation/brackets and non-blind provenance. Those are
not concealed discoveries of this audit. Several concrete descriptions and its broad conclusions
nevertheless need correction. The independent port review confirms the intended mathematics and
exact header counts, with explicit limits on what that textual tool can establish.

After AUD-011, the owner confirmed that a separate round of EasyCrypt changes had finished. This
report separately checks that later state: exact finite-uniform `top2`, failure-returning capped
Theorem 5, corrected verification scripts and qualified publication claims. The original AUD-011
snapshot and its historical findings are preserved; the later additions are not retroactively
credited to it.

**Publication recommendation:** the current EasyCrypt artifact can be presented as checked results
within its prominently stated finite/lossless/ideal-model scope after ordinary packaging decisions.
Lean still needs its failure-masking checker command and its trust/scope descriptions corrected;
either extend its genuine-record experiment or identify the narrower theorem explicitly. Publish
neither package as a proof of exact unrestricted equivalence to all prose. Correct the existing
comparison and port narrative before using them as publication evidence. The two Low findings in
this report are documentary; AUD-012's conditional model/verification blockers remain separate.

## Proposed replacement conclusion for the existing comparison

Both developments check the supplement's principal numerical bounds in distinct formal systems.
Their final theorems have different hypotheses and represented experiment families. The detailed
correspondence table, rather than a blanket equality claim, defines their common scope. In
particular their genuine-record kernels are incomparable, EasyCrypt passphrases remain finite,
Lean's arbitrary-PMF extension covers main deterministic Theorem 5, and some exact ancillary
assemblies are exported in only one development or neither.

Current K5 and W5 match the corrected text. Lean's actual batch and selection-law bridges should
be credited. EasyCrypt's later exact uniform-top2 and capped-failure endpoints should be dated and
bound to their new verified hashes. The independent builds/replays validate each statement in
its own environment; textual port-header comparison is supporting evidence with stated limits.

The two proof engines provide useful cross-checks without blind statement independence or a
formal cross-language equivalence theorem. Neither result certifies concrete primitive costs,
randomness, side channels or the Rust/browser implementation. A public claim should state these
boundaries and supply verification commands that propagate failure and exclude invalid caches.

#### Deniability: disclosed restriction, overstated summary

##### What the comparison correctly says

Lines 284–305 correctly describe the common width-parametric error and its actual private-A/H,
fresh-container, source/key/phrase bad-event and refusal proof route. The current AUD-012 source
review and fresh kernel checks substantiate the corresponding Lean formulas. The theorem is not
merely a guessed footprint estimate: actual adaptive interaction and recovery are represented.

Lines 309–314 explicitly distinguish two real-record models:

- Lean permits the real record to depend on setup, genuine P/E and all four fixed functions, and
  excludes dependence on the realized decoy opening.
- EasyCrypt permits dependence on P/E, genuine/disclosed states, decoy Q and the realized disclosed
  record, but its `dReal` operator does not receive the full oracle functions.

The comparison concludes that neither covers the other and quotes the text's unrestricted usage
condition. This is the same source-level restriction later sharpened in AUD-012-FUN001; it was not
missed by this comparison.

The source confirms the distinction. Historical `DeniabilityGames.lean:1086`–`:1094` and current
lines 1087–1095 sample `realRecords P E tables` before Q and the fresh U record. Historical
`averaged_bound` at line 1445 and current line 1446 keep `usage : Phrase → PMF Record` outside
Setup. EasyCrypt `Deniability.ec:48` gives `dReal` P/E/state/Q/disclosed-state/record arguments,
and line 139 samples it after the disclosed record.

##### What the summary must change

The broad statements at comparison lines 22–23, 582–584 and 732–738 claim that both prove all
numbered results/the same theorems. These need an explicit exception for experimental scope, not
only a list of different constants or a generic “one is more general” caveat. Line 744's assertion
that EasyCrypt's games are the text's games line by line is likewise too literal: the source uses
losslessness requirements, abstract phrase representations, finite passphrase alphabets and
arbitrary first-refusal continuations rather than every operational procedure as written.

A concrete witness makes the Lean record restriction more than a missing parameter name. Fix the
genuine context and condition on distinct real/disclosed phrases. Let U return a fresh fair Boolean.
Allow the genuine record to copy that realized decoy record. Those two records agree with
probability one. Sampling the genuine Boolean before and independently of the fresh U coin gives
agreement probability one half. The `averaged_bound` Setup parameter cannot encode the missing
fresh coin because U is fixed outside Setup. The comparison's own “neither covers the other”
qualification is therefore substantive and should appear in its conclusion.

This witness separates experimental families. It does **not** show that the security bound fails;
the real record is seen only on a lookup already charged in the bad event. Existing pointwise
clean-run equality suggests a straightforward broader formal endpoint. That extension has not

#### Actual capped source experiment

`Selection.ec:767-775` defines `SeqF`. It begins with no success, advances an
integer trial index from -1, asks another independent uniform trial while no
success has occurred and fewer than n trials have been asked, and returns
`Some i` for the first success or `None` otherwise. It is a genuinely
failure-returning rule, rather than the old proof-only `Seq` which returns its
last trial at the internal 2^80 cutoff.

`GameF:779-803` implements the actual capped profile game:

- It initializes the same A/B/H, S, T and Omega lazy tables as `Setup0P`.
- It draws independent P and Q, fixes the generation passphrase to Q, clears
  hidden trial history and calls `SeqF(Trials).select(n)`.
- The trial implementation remains `Passphrase.ec:298-311`: fresh uniform E,
  S(E,Q), T(seed), and the actual 16-bit predicate. Trial entropy, seed and
  T results remain hidden from the adversary.
- On `None`, it returns `None` without constructing a container, calling the
  adversary or calling the creation-refusal continuation. Stale hidden E/seed
  globals on that branch cannot cause a win because final events require
  `res <> None` before testing the output.
- On `Some`, it selects the successful entropy, reads its same seed, encrypts
  under P using the same A/B/H tables, obtains the same Omega reference, then
  either calls the arbitrary refusal response when Y=E or runs the original
  counted attacker on `(Y,omega)`. Its successful output is wrapped in Some.

This matches the specification's fixed stated cap and no-container failure
reading (current DESIGN-NOTES A.6, selection-rule paragraph, and normative
README generation paragraph). It preserves the mandatory creation refusal as
an independently charged branch. A refusal response can be more powerful than
a failure response, which is safe for this upper bound.

The final theorems hold for every integer cap, without a lower-bound premise.
For cap <= 0 the loop makes no trial and the success event is false. Positive
caps may be below, equal to or above the proof's internal 2^80 cutoff; the
coupling explicitly handles all these cases. No performance feasibility of
astronomical integer caps is claimed.

#### Capped transfer and constants

The generic event E in `Selection.ec:809` can inspect the attacker output and
the genuine P/Q/seed. The attacker is restricted from the hidden generation
state/tables, and has its ordinary lossless-oracle hypothesis; the arbitrary

`capped_split:944-1013` proves an event implication by a common-trials
relational coupling against the internal cut game:

1. If a pass occurs within both caps, the same trial is selected, the source

   executes. A refused left creation is covered by the right `DE.r` flag.
2. If the external cap is exhausted first with no pass, the left returns None

3. If the internal 2^80 cutoff is exhausted first with no pass, the right
   cutoff flag is already true. The left may continue to its own finite cap;

top2(m,n) = min(n, min(m, |Pw|) * |Pp|) / (|Pw| * |Pp|),
