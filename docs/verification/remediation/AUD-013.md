## Findings

### AUD-013-DOC001: the comparison's conclusions and several descriptions of Lean needed correction

Confirmed in every point. The places the finding names, by their lines in the reviewed file:

| Lines | What was wrong | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 22-28 (abstract) | "state and prove every numbered result" read as the same theorems | every numbered result has a statement in both; the statements agree on the bounds under their own hypotheses and are not the same theorems; the differences are named |
| 223-229 (4.7) | the selection-rule theorem "applies to" neither the sequential rule nor Theorem 5 | `WalletSelection.chosen_eq` and `actual_eq` are equalities of laws with `WalletCompletion.real` and `WalletUnbounded.real`, from which the game of Theorem 5 draws; what is missing is Theorem 5 with a rule as a parameter |
| 328-329 (5.3) | G4 and G5 summarized without their conditions | `Bad ∧ ¬Comp ∧ ¬Early` in G4; G5 on `¬Early` |
| 376 (5.4) | "no game with batches is defined" | Lean defines batch programs (`BatchProgram.Program`, `run`) and proves the serialization exact (`BatchProgram.execution`, `BatchCrypto.execution`, `BatchCrypto.counts`); `G7.bound` takes a batch adversary; the intermediate claims remain unstated |
| 582-605 (section 6) | "cover every numbered result"; rows on unbounded adversaries, the generation, G7 and the source lengths | the lead says what "not stated" means; the rows are corrected, and rows are added for the real wallet's record, a generation with a limit, the exact `top2`, `top2` as a ranked sum, the simulation without another password, adversaries that may run forever and the check commands |
| 732-744 (conclusions) | "the same theorems"; "the text's games, line by line"; an undetected mistake "unlikely" | agreement of the bounds under each development's hypotheses; what each covers and the other does not; EasyCrypt's games are closer in form, with their limits named; a cross-check of limited independence, with no probability claimed |
| 771 (appendix A) | an empty Lean cell for the 24-word source without wallet data | `G10NoWallet.bound` (WalletData:955) |

The other corrections that the audit's annexes ask for, all made:

- 4.1: not every probabilistic step is a finite sum (strategy mixtures, record distributions,
  passphrase distributions).
- 4.3 and section 6: a Lean program is total and its branching is finite except at record lookups;
  mixtures can run for an unbounded time; what is missing is a theorem that turns an arbitrary
  program halting with probability one into a mixture. Neither development covers an adversary that
  may run forever.
- Section 3: axioms and hypotheses of the statements are separate rows.
- 4.8: the refusal term is `2^-256` for the 256-bit state and `2^-W` in Theorem 2.
- 5.1: neither models a word list, a parser or SHA-256.
- 5.2: the two record models with the witness of a fresh fair bit and the remark on `usage`; the
  explicit failure result of Lean's simulation against EasyCrypt's empty sub-distribution.
- 5.4: the unstated forms with private coins follow on paper, not by a checked statement.
- 5.5: `q_G ≤ 2m` for Lean's attack on the phrase-derived form of G10; the verifier calls of the
  named attacks for a 24-word source.
- 5.11 and section 6: W7's lists of `n + 1` pairs; `top2` on a countable alphabet.
- 4.7 and appendix B, item 9: the rule that reports a failure, the cut rules of the proofs and a
  generation conditioned on its success are three experiments; the sentence on the distribution is
  for fixed functions and passphrase.
- Section 8 and appendix C: the checks of 2026-11-11 are marked as such; the audits of 2026-11-12
  are summarized, with the rule that an EasyCrypt run counts only without compiled caches; the
  check commands are `./check.sh` in both developments.
- Section 10: no theorem links the two developments.
- Head of the document: a table of the states compared on 2026-11-11 and of the states on
  2026-11-12 (text, EasyCrypt, Lean sources, Lean port).

What the revision adds beyond the audit, because the developments changed on 2026-11-12:

- EasyCrypt: `top2_uniform_eq` and Theorem 5 for a generation that stops at a limit
  (`theorem5_seed_capped`, `theorem5_pair_capped`), which the audit checked.
- Lean port: Theorem 2 for a real-wallet record drawn after the disclosed record
  (`Theorem2Records.bits`, chapter `DeniabilityRecords`), added in answer to AUD-012-FUN001 and not
  yet seen by the auditor.

The counts, times and line numbers of sections 1 to 11 are still those of 2026-11-11.

### AUD-013-DOC002: the port account kept stale claims

Confirmed in every point. `PORT-v4.35.md` now says:

- Theorems of the 4.28 inventory: all present except `K5.delta_mono` and the private
  `K5.ranked_allowance`, removed on purpose.
- Size: the figure 618 and 380 counted the non-blank added and removed lines of the patch of that
  time (`grep -c '^+[^+]'`, `'^-[^-]'`); the audit's 660 and 382 count every line of the same patch,
  and 661 and 384 include the later change of the checkpoint's output path. The patch is regenerated
  (`diff -ruN --exclude='*.md'`), with every line counted: 1,028 added and 384 removed in 38 files,
  of which the new chapter `DeniabilityRecords` accounts for 366 lines and its import for one.
- Checkpoint: it writes `checkpoint-inventory.json` to the working directory of the build.
- The header comparison compares normalized text. The account lists what the tools do not compare
  (values of definitions, structure fields, later equation clauses, attributes, inherited
  `variable` lines, strings and comments) and the changes that were read in the sources instead:
  the hypothesis of `FiniteOracleViews`, the names that now resolve to `LegacyPMF`, `ask`, the
  reducibility attributes, `RoundRelabel.tableEquiv` and `Checkpoint.report`.
- `PassphraseRecoding.answers` is a named instance whose name did not change; the tool keys every
  instance by its header, which is why it counts as removed and added.
- Regenerated counts with the new chapter: 2,582 identical, 13 changed, 3 removed, 46 added (30
  before the chapter, which adds 16). `snapshot.tsv` is byte for byte the retained one.

Not changed: `tools/decls.py` and `tools/decldiff.py`. A comparison of elaborated types across the
two toolchains, which the audit suggests for stronger evidence, was not built.
